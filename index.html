Who Was the Zodiac Killer?

The Zodiac Killer is the pseudonym of an unidentified serial killer who murdered at least five people in the San Francisco Bay Area between December 1968 and October 1969. He attacked three couples and a cab driver across Benicia, Vallejo, Napa County, and the city of San Francisco itself — seven victims in total, five of whom died and two of whom survived.

What set him apart from every other killer of his era was not just the violence, but the theater around it: he wrote directly to newspapers, taunted the police, and signed his letters with a symbol resembling the crosshairs of a gun sight — a mark that has since become one of the most recognizable icons in true crime history.

The Known Attacks

December 20, 1968 — David Faraday (17) and Betty Lou Jensen (16) were shot on Lake Herman Road near Benicia, California, in what is considered the first Zodiac attack.

July 4–5, 1969 — Michael Mageau and Darlene Ferrin were shot at Blue Rock Springs Park in Vallejo. Mageau survived; Ferrin did not.

September 27, 1969 — Bryan Hartnell and Cecelia Shepard were stabbed at Lake Berryessa in Napa County. Hartnell survived; Shepard died two days later.

October 11, 1969 — Cab driver Paul Lee Stine was shot and killed in the Presidio Heights neighborhood of San Francisco. A swatch of his bloodied shirt was later mailed to attorney Melvin Belli as proof of the killer's identity.

Letters, Symbols & Ciphers

Between 1969 and 1974, the Zodiac mailed more than twenty letters to Bay Area newspapers — including the San Francisco Chronicle, the San Francisco Examiner, and the Vallejo Times-Herald — as well as to journalist Paul Avery and attorney Melvin Belli. He opened one letter with the chilling line, "This is the Zodiac speaking," and enclosed four cryptograms across his correspondence, famously known as the Z408, Z340, Z32, and Z13 ciphers.

"I am the killer of the 2 teenagers last Christmas at Lake Herman and the girl last 4th of July..." — from the Zodiac's first cipher letter, July 31, 1969

The 408-symbol cipher was cracked within a week by a schoolteacher and his wife in 1969. The notorious 340-character cipher, however, resisted every official and amateur attempt for over 51 years until a team of codebreakers finally solved it in December 2020. Two of his four cryptograms — the Z32 and Z13 — remain unsolved to this day.

The Investigation

Despite one of the largest manhunts in American law enforcement history, the Zodiac was never caught. The only suspect ever named publicly by police was Arthur Leigh Allen, a former elementary school teacher and convicted sex offender who died in 1992 — but no conclusive forensic evidence ever tied him to the crimes.

The case was marked "inactive" by the San Francisco Police Department in 2004, then reopened in 2006. It remains officially open today with the California Department of Justice, the FBI, and multiple county sheriffs' departments, each still hoping DNA technology or an overlooked clue might finally close the file.

Why the Zodiac Still Haunts Us

It's the combination of brilliance and cruelty: a killer who turned murder into a puzzle, who mailed proof of his crimes alongside codes designed to taunt rather than confess. Decades later, amateur sleuths, codebreakers, and investigators around the world continue to chase his identity — and now, so do you.
